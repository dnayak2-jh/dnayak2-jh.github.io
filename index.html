Neural surrogates that stay accurate long after the training window ends.

I work on scientific machine learning for time-dependent PDEs: scalable, reliable, mesh-invariant surrogates that couple neural operators and graph networks with numerical time integration. I am advised by Prof. Somdatta Goswami in Civil and Systems Engineering.

The problem I keep returning to

Autoregressive surrogates accumulate error at every step and drift once they leave the training window. Learning the time derivative and marching it with a numerical integrator keeps the rollout on the true trajectory.

Selected work

CMAME, 2026

TI-DeepONet: learnable time integration for stable long-term extrapolation

Embeds a neural operator inside adaptive numerical time stepping, preserving the Markovian structure of the dynamics. Across six PDE systems, relative L2 extrapolation error drops by about 96.3% against autoregressive rollouts and 83.6% against fixed-horizon training.

Read the paper
CMAME, 2026

PITI-DeepONet: temporal tangent space operator learning

A dual-output DeepONet learns the temporal derivative operator from physics alone or from physics plus data. On four PDE systems it cuts forecasting error by over 79% against full rollout and 84% against autoregressive baselines, with limited or no labeled data.

Read the paper
Results in Engineering, 2026

Data-efficient PDE surrogates: graph neural simulators vs. neural operators

Couples a graph neural simulator with explicit time stepping and reaches under 1% test error from only 3% of the training data. Over five PDE systems, forecasting error falls by over 82% against FNO and 99% against DeepONet autoregressive baselines.

Read the paper
arXiv preprint, 2025

ANCHOR: error-controlled numerical correction for neural operator time marching

A physics-informed residual monitor decides when a neural surrogate hands a step to a high-fidelity solver. Error growth stays bounded under 5 to 10% over long horizons, at up to 10x lower inference cost than the solver alone, on six PDE benchmarks.

Read the preprint
